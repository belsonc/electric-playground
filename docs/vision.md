# Vision

## What is Electric Playground?

Electric Playground is an interactive electronics sandbox inspired by chemistry games like Unreal Chemist.

The shortest description I can think of is:

> **"Unreal Chemist for electricity."**

I've always enjoyed the idea of chemistry sandboxes. When I was younger, I had a chemistry set, and while the instruction book suggested experiments, I was usually more interested in mixing things that weren't supposed to go together just to see what would happen. Games like Unreal Chemist capture that same sense of curiosity while removing the real-world risks.

Over the years I've also developed an interest in electronics—primarily the programming side through projects involving Arduinos, ESP32s, and simple circuits.

That led me to an observation.

Chemistry simulators embrace experimentation. Mix the wrong chemicals together and something interesting happens. Things explode, catch fire, or produce unexpected reactions. Electronics simulators, by comparison, often feel much more clinical. If I wire an LED incorrectly, I'm more likely to receive an error message than to see the consequences of my mistake.

Electric Playground is my attempt to bridge that gap.

The project draws inspiration from tools like Wokwi and Falstad, but its goal is different. It is not trying to become a professional simulator or engineering tool. Instead, it asks a simple question:

> **"What happens if I do this?"**

Suppose a player connects a battery directly to an LED.

The LED pops.

Smoke appears.

The experiment failed—but the player learned something.

They drag a resistor into the circuit.

Now the LED survives.

Curiosity takes over.

"What if I replace the 10Ω resistor with a 220Ω resistor?"

The LED becomes dimmer.

The player has learned something new without reading a lesson or opening a textbook.

That is the experience Electric Playground is trying to create.

Understanding the mathematics and electrical theory should always be available, but it should never be required. A player should be free to enjoy experimenting, noticing patterns, and building intuition before they ever learn words like "Ohm's Law" or "current limiting."

The core values of the project are:

* Curiosity
* Experimentation
* Visual feedback
* Approachability
* Harmless failure
* Learning through discovery

Players should feel comfortable trying ridiculous ideas because failure has no real consequences. If something explodes, they simply reset the experiment and try something else.

Eventually, Electric Playground may offer optional explanations, tooltips, or deeper educational material for players who want to understand the underlying theory. Those features are intentionally secondary.

The first priority is creating a playground where experimentation is enjoyable and where understanding grows naturally through observation.

If players finish a session saying, "I don't know all the equations yet, but I have a pretty good idea what will happen if I change this," then Electric Playground has achieved its goal.
